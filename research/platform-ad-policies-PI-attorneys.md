## 1. META (FACEBOOK/INSTAGRAM) AD POLICIES

### 1.1 Legal Services Classification

Legal services advertising on Meta is treated as a **sensitive/restricted category**. As of 2025-2026, Meta has expanded its Special Ad Categories and tightened rules around legal services, financial products, and other sensitive topics. All ads promoting professional services now require validation through Business Manager — non-compliance may lead to ad account suspension.

**Important**: Legal services ads are subject to BOTH Meta's platform policies AND state bar/ABA advertising rules simultaneously.

---

### 1.2 Claims — What Is Restricted or Prohibited

#### Prohibited Claims:
- **"Guaranteed results"** or **"100% effective"** — promises outcomes that cannot be universally verified
- **"Best lawyer"** or **"best attorney"** — superlative claims without verifiable proof
- **"Instant results"** or time-specific promises (e.g., "results in 24 hours," "overnight transformation")
- Any claim that sounds "too good to be true"
- Deceptive or exaggerated claims about the success of a product or service
- Using the image of a famous person with misleading tactics
- Promising financial benefits by misrepresenting an entity

#### Restricted Claims (allowed with conditions):
- Terms like "best," "guaranteed," or "instant results" may ONLY be used if backed by verifiable proof
- Claims about case results must include qualifying language
- Any statistical claims must be substantiated

#### Recommended Alternatives:
- Replace absolute claims like "guarantees results" with softer language such as "may help," "designed to support," or "has helped clients with..."
- Use factual, verifiable statements about the firm's experience and track record

---

### 1.3 Testimonials, Results, and Guarantees

#### Testimonials:
- Testimonials must NOT promise specific outcomes
- Non-verified testimonials are flagged by Meta's review system
- Before/after testimonials require special care (see section 1.4)
- Testimonials should be real and verifiable
- Must include visible contact information, clear refund/consultation policy, and legal disclaimers where required
- If a testimonial/review states something like "guaranteed a win," do NOT amplify it — it will be seen as misleading

#### Results Claims:
- Past case results CAN be mentioned but MUST include disclaimer language
- Required disclaimer: **"Past results are not a guarantee of future outcomes"** (or state-specific equivalent)
- Cannot promise specific health-related or financial results in specific timeframes without qualifying language

#### Guarantees:
- The word "guaranteed" in connection with case outcomes is effectively PROHIBITED
- "Free consultation" language IS permitted and commonly used
- "No fee unless we win" language is generally permitted but should include appropriate disclaimers

---

### 1.4 Before/After Content

- Before/after layouts or imagery can trigger automated flags even when legitimate
- Before/after photos or non-verified testimonials are flagged by Meta's review system
- This applies especially to health-adjacent content (injury recovery, etc.)
- Restrictions that apply to health/wellness before-and-after content also apply to dramatic results claims in PI contexts
- Ads suggesting "dramatic results" paired with before-and-after images face extra scrutiny

**Best Practice**: Avoid before/after formats entirely in PI ads. If used, ensure robust disclaimers and avoid implying guaranteed outcomes.

---

### 1.5 Disclaimer Requirements

#### Platform-Required:
- Ads must clearly disclose terms, fees, and risks
- Landing pages must be consistent with ad content (Meta evaluates the entire user experience)
- Ads using AI-generated content must disclose AI use (2025 update)
- Starting 2026: companies must clearly declare the source of data used in campaigns

#### State-Bar-Required (must appear in Meta ads):
- Most state bars require: **"This is attorney advertising"**
- Many require: **"Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome"**
- Must include the **name and contact information** of at least one lawyer or law firm responsible for the ad content
- State-specific disclaimer requirements vary — check your jurisdiction

#### Placement:
- Disclaimers must be clearly visible either in the ad's main text OR on the landing page
- For video ads, disclaimers should appear on-screen (not just in audio) since most users watch with sound off

---

### 1.6 Targeting Restrictions

#### Personal Attributes Policy (CRITICAL for PI ads):
Ads **MUST NOT** contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes about the viewer, including:

- Race, ethnicity, or national origin
- Religion or beliefs
- Age
- Sexual orientation or practices
- Gender identity
- **Disability or physical/mental health condition** (directly relevant to PI)
- Vulnerable financial status
- Criminal record
- Name

**What this means for PI ads**: You CANNOT say:
- "Were you injured in a car accident?" (implies knowledge of their injury status)
- "Are you disabled? We can help!" (directly addresses disability)
- "Struggling with medical bills after your accident?" (implies health condition + financial status)
- Any language that assumes the viewer has been in an accident or suffered an injury

**What you CAN say**:
- "If someone you know has been in an accident, our firm may be able to help"
- General informational content about rights after accidents
- Focus on the FIRM'S capabilities rather than the viewer's circumstances
- Use indirect, universal language — let the landing page do the specific targeting

#### Health & Wellness Sensitive Category (January 2025):
- Meta classified health/wellness as a "sensitive category" in January 2025
- Cannot target users based on health conditions or behaviors tied to medical data
- Cannot use purchase/add-to-cart conversion events for optimization in health categories
- PI firms with health-adjacent messaging may be caught in these restrictions
- Businesses may be assessed partial or full restrictions on lower-funnel conversion optimization

#### Special Ad Category Restrictions:
If legal ads are classified under a Special Ad Category:
- Cannot target by specific age (only broad 18-65+ range)
- Cannot target by gender
- Cannot target by ZIP/postal code
- Geographic targeting limited to 15-mile radius minimum
- Detailed demographic and interest-based targeting restricted
- Lookalike Audiences and Saved Audiences restricted
- Customer list custom audiences restricted (as of January 2025 in the US)
- Lead ad forms cannot collect personal attributes like age or gender

### 1.7 Video Format Requirements

#### Aspect Ratios:
| Placement | Recommended Ratio | Resolution |
|-----------|-------------------|------------|
| Feed | 1:1 or 4:5 | 1080x1080 or 1080x1350 |
| Stories | 9:16 | 1080x1920 |
| Reels | 9:16 | 1080x1920 |
| Universal (works everywhere) | 9:16 | 1080x1920 |

- **9:16 vertical** is recommended as the starting point if you want one format across placements
- **4:5** has shown higher click-through rates than square or horizontal on mobile
- Facebook Feed now supports 1080x1920 vertical videos (9:16)

#### Duration:
| Placement | Recommended | Maximum |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Feed | 15-30 seconds | 240 minutes |
| Stories | 15 seconds | 60 seconds |
| Reels | 15-30 seconds | 90 seconds |

#### Technical Specs:
- **File size**: Under 4 GB
- **File types**: MP4, MOV, or GIF
- **Codec**: H.264 video, AAC audio
- **Frame rate**: 30 fps or lower
- **Audio**: Stereo AAC compression at 128 kbps+
- **Pixels**: Square pixels, progressive scan

#### Best Practices:
- Add captions/text overlays — most users scroll with sound muted
- Upload optimized creative per placement (don't rely on Meta auto-cropping)
- Keep key messaging in the first 3 seconds

---

### 1.8 Text-to-Image Ratio Rules

- The old **20% text rule has been REMOVED** (eliminated in 2021)
- No ads will be rejected for having too much text
- HOWEVER: Meta's algorithm still favors images with minimal text
- Meta states: "Images with less than 20% text perform better"
- Ads with excessive text may receive **reduced delivery** and **higher costs**
- The text overlay checking tool has been retired (as of 2023)

**Best Practice**: Continue treating 20% as a guideline even though it's no longer enforced. Less text = better performance = lower CPM.

---

### 1.9 Special Category Rules Summary for Legal Ads

1. Legal services may fall under Special Ad Categories depending on the content
2. All professional services ads require Business Manager validation (2026)
3. Identity verification through Meta is required for certain ad types
4. All ads live in Meta's Ad Library (7 years for political/social issues ads)
5. Meta's AI now auto-flags ads that should be in a Special Ad Category but aren't marked
6. Non-compliance can lead to: ad rejection, account restriction, or permanent ban
7. Stricter ad identity verification system launching in 2026

## 2. TIKTOK AD POLICIES

### 2.1 CRITICAL: Personal Injury Ads Are RESTRICTED on TikTok

**As of October 2025, TikTok explicitly RESTRICTS advertising in the following legal practice areas:**

- **Personal Injury Lawsuits** — car accidents, slip and fall, wrongful death, etc.
- **Family Law** — divorce, custody, alimony, child support, adoption
- **Immigration Law** — green cards, visas, asylum, deportation defense
- **Criminal Law** — DUI, assault, drug charges, criminal defense
- **Employment Law** — wrongful termination, workplace discrimination, wage disputes
- **Legal matters involving children** — any legal services tied to minors

**What IS allowed on TikTok:**
- Educational content about legal topics (purely informational)
- Legal products: tools, resources, software for legal matters (e.g., legal books, legal software, legal courses)
- General law firm brand awareness that does not advertise specific restricted practice areas

**This means: You CANNOT run direct PI attorney advertising on TikTok as paid ads.**

---

### 2.2 Content Restrictions for Attorney Ads (If Running Permitted Legal Ads)

#### Prohibited Content:
- Graphic/bloody/gruesome imagery (crime scenes, accident images, injuries)
- Content depicting violence, self-harm, or behaviors that could lead to injury
- Dismembered, mutilated, charred, or burned human remains
- Shocking or sensational content designed to scare users
- Content that exploits or endorses tragedies
- Misleading or false claims about legal outcomes
- Content promoting illegal, deceptive, or unethical behavior

#### Required:
- Commercial content disclosure is MANDATORY for all promotional posts (2025 update)
- Clear labeling using TikTok's built-in disclosure tools
- Content without proper disclosure may be removed from For You feed or have reach limited

---

### 2.3 Testimonial and Results Claims Rules

- Same general principles as Meta: no guaranteed outcomes, no misleading claims
- Testimonials must not create unrealistic expectations
- No "guaranteed results" or absolute outcome promises
- Educational framing is required — ads must be "purely informative"

---

### 2.4 Disclaimer Requirements

- Commercial content disclosure mandatory via TikTok's built-in tools
- State bar disclaimers still required even in organic/educational content
- FTC regulations apply to all social media advertising including TikTok
- AI-generated content must be disclosed (2025 update)

---

### 2.5 Targeting Restrictions

- TikTok has its own targeting limitations for restricted categories
- The platform-level BAN on PI advertising effectively makes targeting moot for direct PI ads
- For permitted legal ads, standard TikTok targeting applies

---

### 2.6 Video Specs and Requirements

#### In-Feed Ads:
| Spec | Requirement |
|------|------------|
| Aspect Ratio | 9:16 (recommended), 1:1, or 16:9 |
| Resolution | 1080x1920 recommended; minimum 540x960 |
| Duration | 5-60 seconds; **9-15 seconds recommended** |
| File Size | Up to 500 MB |
| File Types | .mp4, .mov, .mpeg, .3gp, .avi |
| Bitrate | Minimum 516 kbps |
| Captions | 12-100 characters for non-Spark ads |

#### TopView Ads:
| Spec | Requirement |
|------|------------|
| Aspect Ratio | 9:16 only |
| Resolution | Minimum 540x960; 1080p recommended |
| Duration | 5-60 seconds; 9-15 seconds recommended |
| File Size | Up to 500 MB |
| Bitrate | Minimum 2,500 kbps |

#### Spark Ads (Boosted Organic):
- No restrictions on ratio, resolution, file type, duration, bitrate, or file size
- Maximum 10 minutes
- Must be uploaded as organic TikTok video first

#### Key Technical Rules:
- No watermarks (including TikTok's own from downloads)
- No imitation of TikTok UI elements
- No emojis, "{ }", or "#" in ad descriptions
- Keep key text/logos clear of the right-side UI and bottom caption bar (safe zone)

---

### 2.7 Organic Content Workaround

While PAID PI ads are restricted, law firms CAN:
- Create organic TikTok content about personal injury topics (educational)
- Build audience through informational content
- Use TikTok Promote to boost organic educational videos (not direct PI advertising)
- Drive traffic to website/landing page where PI services are presented
- This must remain genuinely educational and not be a thinly-veiled direct solicitation

## 3. COMMON COMPLIANCE PITFALLS

### 3.1 State Bar Rules That Intersect with Platform Rules

#### The Dual-Compliance Challenge:
PI attorney ads must comply with BOTH platform policies AND state bar rules simultaneously. Key intersections:

- **ABA Model Rule 7.1**: No false or misleading communications about the lawyer or services
- **ABA Model Rule 7.2**: Advertising rules — must include lawyer/firm name and contact info
- **ABA Model Rule 7.3**: Solicitation rules — restrictions on direct contact
- Social media posts promoting legal services ARE treated as "communications" subject to advertising rules
- Example: "Won another personal injury case. Call me for a free consultation" = regulated advertising

#### State-Specific Traps:

**California — SB 37 (Effective January 2026)**:
- Expands what counts as an "advertisement"
- Tightens content rules on vague promises, flashy awards, and outcome-driven language
- Gives consumers the power to SUE over deceptive attorney marketing
- Compliance is now both an ethics issue AND a litigation risk

**Florida**:
- Rule 4-7.11: Lawyer advertising rules apply to press releases
- Using a "parallel law firm" name like "The Personal Injury Firm" violates Rule 4-7.21 if it implies exclusive practice in PI when they don't
- Ads are actively monitored

**Alabama (Effective January 2026)**:
- Comprehensive overhaul of Rules 7.1, 7.2, and 7.3
- Direct email/letter to known injury victims: only allowed 21+ days after the accident

**Texas**:
- Uninvited solicitation by ANY means (phone, mail, email, "friending," tweeting) = "barratry"
- Barratry is a **third-degree felony** in Texas
- Extreme caution required with social media outreach

**New York**:
- 30-day moratorium on solicitation after an accident
- Specific disclaimer requirements for attorney advertising

#### Key Principle:
Firms must know the rules for EVERY state where they advertise, not just their home state. Multi-state advertising requires multi-state compliance.

---

### 3.2 Language That Triggers Rejections

#### Meta Rejection Triggers:
1. **"Were you injured in a [type of] accident?"** — violates personal attributes policy
2. **"Guaranteed results"** or **"guaranteed settlement"** — misleading claims
3. **"Best lawyer"** or **"#1 attorney"** — unverifiable superlative claims
4. **"We will get you money"** or **"We will win your case"** — outcome guarantees
5. **"You deserve compensation"** — may imply personal knowledge of viewer's situation
6. **"Struggling with medical bills?"** — implies health condition + financial status
7. **"Are you disabled?"** — directly addresses personal attributes
8. **"Instant relief"** or **"Fast settlement"** — time-specific promises
9. Any language suggesting the viewer HAS BEEN in an accident (vs. general awareness)

#### Safer Alternatives:
| Risky Language | Safer Alternative |
|---------------|-------------------|
| "Were you hurt in an accident?" | "Accident victims may have legal rights" |
| "We guarantee results" | "Our firm has recovered $X million for clients" |
| "Best PI lawyer in [city]" | "Experienced personal injury attorneys serving [city]" |
| "We'll get you the money you deserve" | "We fight for fair compensation for our clients" |
| "Call now — you're running out of time" | "Statutes of limitations may apply — learn more" |
| "Struggling with medical bills?" | "Medical expenses after an accident can add up" |

---

### 3.3 Guarantee Language Restrictions

**Across ALL platforms and state bars:**
- NEVER guarantee a specific dollar amount
- NEVER guarantee a specific outcome (win, settlement, verdict)
- NEVER guarantee a specific timeline for resolution
- NEVER use "guaranteed" in connection with legal outcomes
- "No fee unless we win/recover" IS generally permitted (it's a fee structure, not an outcome guarantee) — but must be accurate

**Permitted guarantee-adjacent language:**
- "Free consultation" — widely accepted
- "No fee unless we recover for you" — permitted in most jurisdictions with proper disclaimers
- "We don't get paid unless you do" — permitted in most jurisdictions
- "Contingency fee basis" — factual statement of fee structure

---

### 3.4 Before/After Restrictions

**Meta:**
- Before/after formats are flagged by automated review systems
- Especially scrutinized in health-adjacent contexts
- Before/after injury recovery images will likely trigger flags
- Before/after settlement amount comparisons ("offered $5K, we got $500K") need heavy disclaimers

**TikTok:**
- Before/after in the context of PI is essentially moot since PI ads are restricted
- For organic content, before/after injury images would violate graphic content policies

**State Bars:**
- Many states require "Prior results do not guarantee similar outcome" with any results comparison
- Some states prohibit dramatizations unless labeled as such
- Re-enactments must be clearly identified

---

### 3.5 Sensitive Content Policies (Accident Imagery, Injuries)

#### Meta:
- **"Ads must not contain shocking, sensational, disrespectful, or excessively violent content"**
- Accident scene photos: RISKY — may be flagged as sensational content
- Injury photos: LIKELY REJECTED — graphic/shocking imagery
- Hospital/medical imagery: USE WITH CAUTION — may trigger health/wellness restrictions
- Car wreck imagery: MODERATE RISK — commonly used but may trigger flags depending on severity
- Stock photos of accidents: LOWER RISK but still subject to sensational content policy

**What works on Meta:**
- Professional footage of the attorneys/firm
- Tasteful, non-graphic imagery (e.g., someone with a minor visible injury like a sling or bandage, NOT open wounds)
- Courtroom or legal-themed imagery
- Client testimonial videos (with proper disclaimers)
- Educational/informational content about legal rights

#### TikTok:
- ALL graphic imagery of accidents, injuries, crime scenes = BANNED
- Dismembered, mutilated, charred, burned imagery = BANNED
- Even "mild" accident imagery may be flagged
- Content exploiting tragedies = BANNED
- Essentially: no accident/injury imagery AT ALL in TikTok ads

---

### 3.6 Personal Attributes Targeting Restrictions

**This is the #1 compliance issue for PI attorneys on Meta.**

#### The Rule:
Ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes. You cannot create an ad that assumes the viewer:
- Has been injured
- Has a disability
- Has a health condition
- Is in financial distress
- Has been in an accident
- Is involved in a legal matter

#### Why It Matters for PI:
The entire premise of PI advertising is reaching people who have been injured. Meta's personal attributes policy directly conflicts with this goal. You must thread the needle:

**PROHIBITED approach**: "If you were injured in a car accident, call us"
**PERMITTED approach**: "Car accident injuries can be devastating. [Firm name] has helped thousands recover compensation. Learn your legal rights."

The key distinction: Speak ABOUT situations in general, don't speak TO the viewer about THEIR situation.

#### Exception — Public Service Announcements:
Advertisers MAY make PSAs that inform the public about issues related to personal health attributes, as long as they don't assert that someone or their family HAS a health condition. This creates an avenue for educational PI content framed as public information.

---

### 3.7 Additional 2025-2026 Compliance Considerations

#### AI-Generated Content:
- Both Meta and TikTok now require disclosure of AI-generated content
- Meta may reject or reduce reach of ads without AI disclosure
- AI-generated images depicting photorealistic scenarios must be labeled

#### Community Notes (Meta):
- Meta replaced third-party fact-checking with Community Notes in the US
- Legal ads could be annotated by community members
- Claims in ads may face public scrutiny through this system
- More reason to ensure all claims are accurate and well-disclaimed

#### Data Privacy (2026):
- Meta will require declaration of data sources used in campaigns
- Stricter identity verification for advertisers
- Cross-platform tracking documentation required

#### Enforcement Trends:
- Meta uses AI-driven moderation (may over-flag legitimate PI content)
- Both platforms can: reject ads, restrict accounts, or permanently ban
- 35 state AGs have called on Meta to remove deceptive ads — signaling increased scrutiny
- PI firms (high-stakes, vulnerable clients) may be watched more closely by state bars

## PRACTICAL SUMMARY: WHAT PI FIRMS CAN AND CANNOT DO

### META (Facebook/Instagram) — CAN DO:
- Run PI attorney ads with proper compliance
- Use testimonials with disclaimers
- Mention case results with "past results do not guarantee..." disclaimer
- Use professional, non-graphic imagery
- Target by geography (15-mile minimum if Special Ad Category)
- Run video ads in 9:16, 4:5, or 1:1 formats
- Use "free consultation" and contingency fee language
- Run educational/informational content about legal rights

### META — CANNOT DO:
- Guarantee outcomes or specific dollar amounts
- Use language implying knowledge of viewer's personal situation
- Show graphic accident/injury imagery
- Target by health condition, disability, or personal attributes
- Use before/after without heavy disclaimers
- Make unverifiable superlative claims
- Omit required state bar disclaimers

### TIKTOK — CAN DO:
- Create organic educational content about PI topics
- Boost educational content with TikTok Promote
- Build brand awareness through informational videos
- Advertise legal tools, resources, and educational products
- Drive traffic to website through educational content

### TIKTOK — CANNOT DO:
- Run paid ads for PI legal services (explicitly restricted)
- Run paid ads for family law, immigration, criminal, or employment law
- Use any graphic accident or injury imagery
- Make guaranteed outcome claims
- Run direct solicitation ads for PI cases
